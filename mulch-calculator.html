How much mulch do I need?

Mulch is figured the same way as any ground cover — bed area times depth — but unlike gravel it's light, so it's sold by volume, not weight. The two ways to buy it are bulk (by the cubic yard, delivered in a pile) or bagged (usually 2 cubic foot bags), and the calculator gives you both counts plus the cost each way so you can see where the crossover is. Depth is the lever that matters most: 2 inches versus 3 inches is a 50% difference in how much you buy.

How to measure your beds

Measure each bed's length and width in feet — for curved or island beds, approximate with a rectangle, circle, or triangle and add them together. Then choose your depth. A couple of practical notes:

  • Refreshing vs. new. Topping up existing mulch usually needs only 1–2 inches. A brand-new bed, or one where you want real weed suppression, wants 3 inches.
  • Don't overdo depth. More than 3–4 inches, or mulch piled against stems and trunks ("volcano mulching"), traps moisture and can harm plants. Deeper isn't better here.
Worked example. Beds totaling 200 sq ft at 3 inches = 200 × 0.25 ft = 50 ft³ = 1.85 cubic yards, or about 25 two-cubic-foot bags. Add 10% and you'd grab ~28 bags — or order 2 yards bulk, which at typical prices is cheaper at this size.

Bags, pallets, or bulk?

This is the real decision, and the calculator flags it. Bagged mulch is convenient and clean for small jobs; bulk is cheaper per yard once you need a lot. The rough guide:

  • Under ~1.5 cubic yards (about 20 bags) — buy bags. No delivery fee, easy to handle, and you only open what you need.
  • Around 1.5–3 yards — compare. A pallet of bags (~65 bags ≈ 4.8 yd³) or a bulk delivery both work; bulk usually edges ahead on price.
  • Over ~3 yards — order bulk. It's noticeably cheaper by volume and one dumped pile beats hauling and splitting 40+ bags.

Mulch coverage by depth

How far mulch spreads depends entirely on depth:

A cubic yard = 27 ft³, so it takes about 13.5 standard 2 ft³ bags to equal one cubic yard (round up to 14). A pallet is usually ~65 bags, roughly 4.8 cubic yards.

What mulch costs

Bagged mulch typically runs $3–$5 per 2 cubic foot bag, so a cubic yard in bags is roughly $40–$70. Bulk mulch is usually $30–$45 per cubic yard plus a delivery fee, which is why bulk wins on larger jobs. Dyed and premium mulches (cypress, cedar) cost more than plain hardwood. The calculator estimates material only; delivery and spreading, if you hire it out, are separate.

Frequently asked questions

How many bags of mulch are in a yard?

About 13.5 standard 2 cubic foot bags, since a cubic yard is 27 cubic feet — round up to 14. If you're using 3 cubic foot bags, it takes 9 to make a cubic yard.

How much mulch do I need?

Multiply bed length × width × depth in feet and divide by 27 for cubic yards. Most beds want 2–3 inches. A 200 sq ft bed at 3 inches needs about 1.85 cubic yards, or 25 two-cubic-foot bags.

How deep should mulch be?

2–3 inches is ideal for most beds; 3–4 inches for new beds or heavy weed suppression. Keep mulch off stems and trunks, and don't exceed about 4 inches — deeper can suffocate roots and hold too much moisture.

How many bags of mulch are on a pallet?

Usually about 65 two-cubic-foot bags, which is roughly 4.8 cubic yards, though it varies by brand and mulch type. Past a pallet or two, bulk delivery is normally cheaper.

Is bagged or bulk mulch cheaper?

Bulk is cheaper per yard once you need more than about 1.5–2 cubic yards, even with a delivery fee. For small beds, bags win because there's no delivery cost and no leftover pile.
