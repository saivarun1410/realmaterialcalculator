How much gravel do I need?

Gravel is figured by volume — area times depth — and then usually bought by weight, which is where it differs from concrete. Suppliers quote either cubic yards or tons, so the calculator gives you both plus the cost each way. The one number that quietly controls everything is depth: a driveway at 4 inches needs twice the gravel of a 2-inch walkway over the same area, so nail that down first.

How to measure your area

Measure length and width in feet for a rectangle; for round areas use the diameter, and for odd shapes break them into rectangles and triangles and add them up. Then pick your depth in inches. Two things to keep in mind:

  • Order by the ton for anything bulk. A pickup bed holds only about 1 ton safely, so a real driveway means delivery — and delivery is priced by the ton at most quarries.
  • Account for compaction. Gravel is delivered loose and settles once it's spread and driven on. The overage slider covers this; 10% is a sensible default, more for a soft or uneven base.
Worked example — a gravel driveway. A 40 ft × 12 ft driveway at 4 inches = 480 sq ft × 0.333 ft = 160 ft³ = 5.93 cubic yards. In crushed stone (1.40 t/yd³) that's about 8.3 tons. Add 10% overage and you'd order roughly 6.5 yd³ / 9 tons.

Tons or cubic yards? (the density part)

This trips people up because gravel is sold both ways and the conversion isn't fixed — it depends on the material's density. As a rule of thumb, one cubic yard is about 1.4 tons for common crushed stone and pea gravel. Heavier, denser materials weigh more:

What gravel costs

Bulk gravel typically runs about $30–$60 per ton or $40–$80 per cubic yard at the supplier, before delivery. Decorative stone (river rock, colored gravel) costs more; plain crusher run is cheapest. Delivery is usually a flat trip fee plus a per-ton rate, so bigger single orders are cheaper per ton. For a full driveway installed — grading, base, and gravel — expect a contractor quote rather than a material-only figure, which is what the "get quotes" option above is for.

Frequently asked questions

How many tons of gravel are in a cubic yard?

About 1.4 tons for most crushed stone and pea gravel. Denser materials like crusher run and paver base are closer to 1.5 tons per yard, and sand is around 1.35. In pounds, a cubic yard runs roughly 2,600–3,000 lb depending on material and moisture.

How much gravel do I need for a driveway?

Multiply length × width × depth (all in feet) and divide by 27 for cubic yards. Driveways are usually 4–6 inches of gravel. A 12 × 40 ft driveway at 4 inches needs about 5.9 cubic yards — roughly 8 tons of crushed stone.

How deep should gravel be for a driveway?

Aim for 4–6 inches of surface gravel. On soft ground, build in layers: a 4-inch base of larger stone, then 3–4 inches of finer crusher run on top, for 8–10 inches total.

How much area does a ton of gravel cover?

Roughly 100 sq ft at 2 inches, 65–70 sq ft at 3 inches, or 50 sq ft at 4 inches. A ton is about 0.7 cubic yards, so coverage falls as depth rises.

How much gravel do I need for a paver base?

A paver base is typically 4–6 inches of compacted crusher run or road base under 1 inch of bedding sand. Use the calculator with "paver base" selected and a 4–6 inch depth for the stone layer, then figure the sand separately.
