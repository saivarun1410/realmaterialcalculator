How to measure for concrete

The calculator is only as accurate as your measurements, and concrete is unforgiving — you can't order "a bit more" once the truck leaves. Measure length and width in feet, and thickness in inches (most people get this wrong by mixing units). For a slab, thickness is the depth of the pour, not the depth of your excavation.

Three things quietly inflate real-world volume beyond the clean geometric number:

The over-dig problem. If your subgrade isn't level, concrete fills every low spot. A slab formed at 4 inches can easily average 4.5–5 inches over an uneven base — that's 10–25% more concrete than the flat calculation. Compact and screed your base flat, or bump the waste allowance to 15%.

For footings and columns, measure the actual form dimensions, not the hole. For round tube forms (Sonotube), the diameter printed on the tube is the finished diameter — use that. And if you're pouring several identical footings or columns, set the quantity field rather than adding them up by hand.

Worked example: a 10 × 12 ft patio

Area: 10 ft × 12 ft = 120 sq ft
Thickness: 4 in = 0.333 ft
Volume: 120 × 0.333 = 40 cubic feet
Cubic yards: 40 ÷ 27 = 1.48 cu yd
+ 10% waste: 1.63 cu yd
→ Order 1.75 cu yd of ready-mix (round up to the supplier's increment)

At 1.6+ cubic yards this is well past bag territory — it would take about 70 eighty-pound bags, over 5,000 lb of mix to haul, open, and mix by hand. Ordering ready-mix is cheaper and saves a brutal day of labor. Which brings up the decision most guides skip:

Bags vs. ready-mix: where the line actually is

The honest threshold is about 1 cubic yard. Below it, bags win on convenience and there's no delivery minimum to worry about. Above it, ready-mix wins on both cost and effort — but watch for the short-load fee: most suppliers charge a premium on deliveries under 1 cubic yard, sometimes $60–150, which can erase the savings on very small pours. So the awkward middle — roughly 0.7 to 1.5 cubic yards — is where you should price both.

Rule of thumb: under ~40 eighty-pound bags, buy bags. Over ~60, order ready-mix. In between, get a delivery quote including any short-load fee and compare.

What concrete costs

Two very different price structures, so estimate whichever route you're taking:

Ready-mix runs roughly $125–165 per cubic yard delivered in most of the US, before add-ons. The add-ons are where budgets slip: short-load fees under 1 cu yd, an overtime/Saturday surcharge, and a waiting-time fee if your crew is slow to unload. Bagged mix runs about $5–8 per 80 lb bag, so a cubic yard's worth of bags (45 of them) lands near $225–360 — often more than ready-mix per yard, which is the counterintuitive reason bags only make sense on small jobs.

Prices vary a lot by region and by month — the calculator's cost fields are editable, so drop in a real local quote for an accurate number.

Frequently asked questions

How many 80 lb bags of concrete are in a cubic yard?

45 bags. An 80 lb bag yields about 0.6 cubic feet, and a cubic yard is 27 cubic feet (27 ÷ 0.6 = 45). It takes 60 sixty-pound bags or 90 forty-pound bags to fill the same cubic yard.

How much concrete do I need for a 10×10 slab at 4 inches?

About 1.23 cubic yards. That's 100 sq ft × 0.333 ft = 33.3 cubic feet, divided by 27. Add 10% for waste and order roughly 1.36 cubic yards. At this size ready-mix beats bags on both cost and labor.

Should I use bags or ready-mix?

Under about 1 cubic yard (roughly 40–60 eighty-pound bags) bags are practical. Above that, ready-mix delivery is usually cheaper and far less work — just check for a short-load fee on deliveries under a cubic yard.

How thick should a concrete slab be?

4 inches is standard for patios, walkways, and shed floors. Step up to 5–6 inches for driveways or anything that carries vehicle weight, and add rebar or wire mesh for those loads.

How much extra should I order for waste?

Add 5–10%. Spillage, an uneven subgrade, and slight over-digging all consume more than the exact geometric volume. This calculator defaults to 10%; use 15% if your base isn't well compacted and screeded.
